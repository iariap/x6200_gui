# Capture filters — SpeexDSP NR + AF leveler on the captured RX PCM

This document describes what the audio-enhancement module **actually does** on the radio
today, what it does **not** do, and which numbers are measured versus unknown. It replaces
the earlier aspirational text: claims about processed audio reaching the speaker, about
"professional-grade" quality or about comparison with other radios were never measured and
are not repeated here.

Evidence for the current behaviour lives in:

- [`docs/evidencia/2026-10-28-rx-audio-feasibility/`](evidencia/2026-10-28-rx-audio-feasibility/)
  ([#345](https://github.com/iariap/x6200_gui/issues/345)) — signal-flow map (E1–E15), audit of
  the old chain, and the pinned SpeexDSP bench.
- [`docs/evidencia/2026-10-28-rx-audio-hardware/`](evidencia/2026-10-28-rx-audio-hardware/)
  ([#347](https://github.com/iariap/x6200_gui/issues/347)) — RX-only hardware gate: the
  audible return path is `NOT_VERIFIED` / `NO_GO_LIVE`.
- [`docs/evidencia/2026-10-29-audio-capture-351/`](evidencia/2026-10-29-audio-capture-351/)
  ([#351](https://github.com/iariap/x6200_gui/issues/351)) — bench of **this** revision against
  real `libspeexdsp`, with `measurements.csv` and `commands.log`.

## 1. Scope: the capture copy only

`src/audio.c::read_callback()` captures `alsa_input.platform-sound.stereo-fallback` as
**mono S16NE at 44 100 Hz** (`AUDIO_CAPTURE_RATE` in `src/audio.h`). On that fragment it does,
in order and exactly once per sample:

1. `memcpy()` into a stack scratch buffer (the fragment memory belongs to PulseAudio and is
   never written);
2. `audio_enh_process(scratch, count)` — the chain documented below;
3. `screen_rec_put_audio_samples(count, scratch)` — Recorder (AVI/MP3);
4. `dsp_put_audio_samples(count, scratch)` — CW/RTTY/FT8/JS8/… decoders and the spectrum.

That is the **only** destination of the processed PCM. The speaker/headphone output is driven
by the BASE, and `src/audio.c::audio_play()` is a **separate** path for audio generated by
apps (voice prompts, digital-mode playback) that writes to `play_stm`. The capture filters are
never inserted into `audio_play()`, into the mixer, into A2DP (`rootfs/usr/sbin/btaudio-ctl`
runs its own `module-loopback`) or into TX.

> **Never the speaker.** No stream is added, no loopback is created, no `record_set`, mic,
> `spmode` or PTT register is touched by this module. Whether a *safe* processed-RX → speaker
> route exists at all is still `NOT_VERIFIED` (see #347); this document does not assume one.

## 2. Rates and frames (real values)

| Item | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Capture rate | 44 100 Hz, mono, S16NE | `src/audio.h::AUDIO_CAPTURE_RATE` |
| SpeexDSP rate | 44 100 Hz | `AUDIO_ENH_SAMPLE_RATE` |
| NR frame | **441 samples = 10 ms** | `AUDIO_ENH_FRAME_SIZE` |
| Capture scratch | 4410 samples (100 ms) in the stack | `CAPTURE_SCRATCH_SAMPLES` |
| Resampling | none | — |

`src/audio_enhancement.c` has two `#error` guards: `AUDIO_ENH_SAMPLE_RATE` must equal
`AUDIO_CAPTURE_RATE`, and `AUDIO_ENH_FRAME_SIZE` must equal `AUDIO_CAPTURE_RATE / 100`. If
someone changes the capture rate, the firmware stops compiling instead of feeding Speex a
false rate. The old constants (48 000 Hz / 256 samples) were wrong for this stream and were
the source of the 58-sample truncation measured in #345.

## 3. Processing chain

```
PCM captured (mono S16, 44.1 kHz)
        │
        ├─ notch filters        (API only: no UI, no caller in the firmware)
        ├─ AF leveler           (per block, one log10f/powf per block)
        ├─ SpeexDSP NR          (stateful, 441-sample frames)
        │
        └─ S16 out  →  Recorder + decoders (dsp_put_audio_samples)
```

Order is fixed: **notch → AF leveler → Speex NR**. The chain is **length-preserving**: exactly
N samples out for N samples in, for every N (1, 440, 441, 442, 257, 4410, 8820, …). The old
behaviour (only complete 256-sample frames processed, the tail passed through raw) is gone;
there is no raw tail per callback any more.

### 3.1 NR state machine and the 10 ms priming

The NR stage keeps `in_frame[441]`, `in_count`, `out_frame[441]`, `out_index` and `out_ready`
in `audio_enh_state_t`. For each **pre-NR** sample (after notch and leveler) it:

1. emits the next sample of the previously completed frame, or `0` while no frame is ready
   yet — this is the **priming: exactly 441 samples (10 ms) of silence**;
2. appends the current sample to `in_frame`;
3. when 441 samples are gathered, runs **one** `speex_preprocess_run()` and turns that frame
   into the next `out_frame`.

Consequences:

- the delay with NR on is **constant**, and it was measured with the real library as
  **882 samples = 20 ms**: 441 samples from the frame machine (a frame is emitted one frame
  late) **plus 441 samples of algorithmic delay inside `libspeexdsp` itself** (the
  preprocessor works on an overlapped window, so its output trails its input by one frame).
  #345 had already measured that library delay as exactly one frame (5.805 ms at 256-sample
  frames); at 441-sample frames it is 10 ms. The two parts are separated by the
  `probe_speex_only` row of the bench (a bare `SpeexPreprocessState`, no module code):
  **441 samples** there, **882 samples** through `audio_enh_process()`;
- the **priming happens once**, when the NR machine starts from a reset state. It is not
  repeated per callback and it is not applied per chunk;
- the priming and the delay live **only in the capture copy**. They do not exist on the
  speaker path (not processed here) and not on TX (not processed here);
- `Off` and `Digital` are **bit-exact passthrough** (the chain is disabled: no priming, no
  delay, output == input);
- `CW weak` keeps NR disabled (tone preserved) and only runs the AF leveler, so it is also
  passthrough for the NR stage.

### 3.2 AF leveler

Enabled only in `CW weak` and `AM broadcast`. It measures the **peak of the block it receives**,
and `audio_enh_process()` splits every callback into chunks of **at most 441 samples**, so the
measurement window is 441 samples unless a partition forces shorter chunks. The gain is smoothed
with attack/release and clamped to ±30 dB. Measured consequence: `AM broadcast` produces the
**same output** for every partition that keeps 441-sample chunks (4410, 441 and 8820 give the
same SHA-256) and differs only when a partition forces shorter chunks (`1,440,441,442,257`
differs in 429 869 samples, max abs diff 1103 LSB). That is why the issue does not require
bit-exactness for `AM broadcast` — and why it *is* required for `Off` and `SSB voice`, which
have the leveler disabled; see §6.

### 3.3 Notch filters

`audio_enh_notch_add/remove/clear_all/set_freq/set_bw` exist as an API over liquid-dsp
`firfilt_rrrf_create_notch()`. **There is no UI and no caller in the firmware**: no notch is
ever created today. There is no notch screen, no notch indicator and no notch shortcut — the
earlier text describing a notch UI was wrong. The bench aborts if a notch is created, so no
measurement can silently "prove" a notch that does not exist.

## 4. Presets (persisted values unchanged)

The Settings row is **`Capture filters`** (formerly labelled `Audio enhance`). The persisted
parameter is still `params.audio_enh_preset` (DB name literal, unchanged), range 0–4, and the
five options are exactly, in this order:

| Index | Option | NR (Speex) | NR level | AF leveler | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `Off` | off | — | off | chain disabled; bit-exact passthrough |
| 1 | `CW weak` | off | — | on, target −25 dB, medium | tone preserved; NR deliberately off |
| 2 | `SSB voice` | **on** | **40** | off | relies on the BASE hardware AGC |
| 3 | `AM broadcast` | **on** | **60** | on, target −18 dB, slow | leveler + stronger NR |
| 4 | `Digital` | off | — | off | chain disabled; bit-exact passthrough |

Indices, order and serialization are part of the contract and were not changed by the relabel.
`40` and `60` are the stored preset levels; there is **no new user level control**.

**CW/CWR override.** In CW and CWR the *effective* profile is the CW profile (NR off) unless
the user selected `Off` or `Digital`. This is the rule from PR #232: the user's preference in
`params.audio_enh_preset` is **not** modified, only the processing. This was measured on the
radio: with `SSB voice` selected in CW, the capture stayed continuous (441 000 bytes in
exactly 5 s) but the envelope had 12.9 % of blocks below half the median — the NR/leveler
pumping masked the tone for the decoder.

## 5. Noise-suppression level polarity

`audio_enh_nr_set_level(uint8_t level)` maps 0–100 to the SpeexDSP control
`SPEEX_PREPROCESS_SET_NOISE_SUPPRESS` (real ABI constant **18**) as:

```
noise_suppress_dB = -10 - (clamp(level, 0, 100) * 20) / 100
```

| level | 0 | 40 | 60 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| requested dB | −10 | −18 | −22 | −30 |

More level = **more** attenuation (more negative), which is the polarity Speex documents.
The previous formula (`-30 + 20*level/100`) was inverted: asking for maximum gave the mildest
suppression, and the −30 dB end was unreachable. The mapping is verified against the real
library by reading `SPEEX_PREPROCESS_GET_NOISE_SUPPRESS` back from the state, and by measuring
the RMS attenuation of white noise — see the bench in §6. This correction does **not** by
itself claim better voice quality; it only makes the control mean what it says.

## 6. Measured behaviour (host bench, real SpeexDSP)

Bench: [`docs/evidencia/2026-10-29-audio-capture-351/`](evidencia/2026-10-29-audio-capture-351/)
(`scripts/bench_capture.py`, `measurements.csv`, `commands.log`). It compiles
`src/audio_enhancement.c` **verbatim** against real `libspeexdsp-dev=1.2.1-1` inside a
`debian:bookworm-slim` image pinned by digest; only the periphery (LVGL tick, cfg subjects,
params, liquid-dsp) is stubbed, and liquid-dsp aborts if called. Signals are synthetic and
deterministic (`gen_pcm.py`, seed 6200, 10 s) — tones and noise, **not** voice.

What the bench records per case: rate, frame, effective suppression level (read back from the
real state), delay by cross-correlation, RMS/peak, clipping, callback-boundary seams, process
time, and samples produced (N out vs N in). What it **cannot** measure: audible quality,
intelligibility, the RX→speaker route and the CPU cost on the R16. Those stay `BLOCKED`
(#345, #347).

Measured on 10 s of deterministic white noise at −30 dBFS (seed 6200), 44 100 Hz, frame 441,
callbacks of 4410 samples, `libspeexdsp 1.2.1-1`:

| Preset | Delay (correlation) | RMS attenuation | Clipping | Max sample-to-sample jump: input / at callback boundary / at frame boundary | 10 s of audio in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Off` | 0 samples | 0.00 dB (bit-exact) | 0 | 3587 / 3189 / 3466 LSB | ≈1 ms |
| `SSB voice` | **882 samples (20 ms)** | −11.86 dB | 0 | 3587 / 3336 / 3336 LSB | ≈26–28 ms |
| `AM broadcast` | **882 samples (20 ms)** | −9.32 dB | 0 | 3587 / 3727 / 3727 LSB | ≈28 ms |

Reading the seams: with `SSB voice` the largest jump in the output at a callback boundary
(and at a frame boundary) is **smaller** than the largest jump the input already has, i.e. the
partitioning introduces no discontinuity of its own. With `AM broadcast` it is slightly
**larger** (3727 vs 3587 LSB, +140 LSB ≈ 4 % of the input's own largest jump), at the 441-sample
grid: that is the AF leveler changing gain once per block, and it is reported here rather than
hidden. The 882-sample delay decomposes into 441 (frame machine) + 441 (inside `libspeexdsp`),
as measured by the `probe_speex_only` row.

Partition invariance: the same input processed as one 4410-sample callback, as 441-sample
callbacks, as `8820`, and as the arbitrary pattern `1,440,441,442,257` gives the **same
SHA-256** for `Off` and for `SSB voice`. For `AM broadcast` the first three partitions are also
identical to each other, but the arbitrary pattern differs (429 869 differing samples, max abs
diff 1103 LSB against the 4410 partition): the AF leveler measures the peak of the block it
receives, and `audio_enh_process()` splits any callback into chunks of at most 441 samples, so
only a partition that forces shorter chunks changes the gain. That is why the issue does not
require bit-exactness for AM — and the bench records the difference instead of hiding it.

The process times are host x86_64 wall-clock numbers for 10 s of audio (≈0.25 % of real time
for the NR presets) and vary between host runs; the exact values of the recorded run are in
`measurements.csv`. They are **not** a CPU budget for the R16.

## 7. State reset rules

The NR machine (`in_frame`, `out_frame`, indices, `out_ready`) is discarded — never persisted,
never replayed — on:

- chain ON↔OFF (`audio_enh_set_enabled()`),
- NR ON↔OFF (`audio_enh_nr_set_enabled()`),
- changing the effective preset (`audio_enh_preset_load()`, `audio_enh_preset_reset()`),
- a PulseAudio **hole** (`audio_enh_nr_reset_pending()` from the capture callback),
- `audio_enh_init()` / `audio_enh_deinit()`.

A reset discards at most the pending internal state. Old audio never leaks into the new mode,
and the preset stored in `params.audio_enh_preset` is never touched by a reset.

**What a preset reset does not reset (measured with the real library).** The reset above
discards the frame machine's pending buffers. It does **not** destroy and recreate the
`SpeexPreprocessState`, so `libspeexdsp` keeps its **adaptive noise estimate** (and the AF
leveler keeps its smoothed gain). Consequence, measured in the bench: processing the same
input twice in one process with `audio_enh_preset_reset()` in between is **not** bit-exact
for `SSB voice`/`AM broadcast` (433 735 and 438 096 differing samples, max abs diff 1539/1580
LSB on white noise). Bit-exactness across partitions is therefore stated for a **freshly
initialised state** (`audio_enh_init()` + preset load), which is the state the contract fixes;
`Off` stays bit-exact even after a reset. This is reported as measured; restarting the Speex
adaptation on a preset change would require re-creating the state, which this revision does
not do.

## 8. Concurrency and startup

- One static `pthread_mutex_t` serialises **all** accesses to the effective state (Speex
  `run`/`ctl`/`destroy`, notches, AF leveler, enable/preset/NR). `audio_enh_process()` holds it
  while processing; setters/getters/preset/mode/clear/reset/deinit take it on access. Internal
  `*_locked` helpers avoid recursive locking (the mutex is not recursive).
- The module lock is never held while calling LVGL APIs, the PulseAudio mainloop,
  `x6200_control_*`, `params_flush` or UI hooks. `lv_tick_get()` and `params`/Subjects are read
  **before** taking it.
- `audio_enh_init()` is called **synchronously and once** in `audio_init()`, before the
  background PulseAudio/`amixer` thread is created. A late init can no longer overwrite a
  preset the user already applied; `dsp_init()` re-applies the effective preset after
  `params_init()`.

## 9. Capture callback contract (why the copy is safe)

`read_callback()` treats `pa_stream_peek()` according to the documented contract:

| `pa_stream_peek()` | Action |
|---|---|
| `rc < 0` | log, return; **no** `pa_stream_drop()` |
| `rc == 0`, `data == NULL`, `nbytes == 0` | empty: return silently, no drop |
| `rc == 0`, `data == NULL`, `nbytes > 0` | hole: `audio_enh_nr_reset_pending()`, deliver **zeros** through both consumers **without** processing, one drop |
| `rc == 0`, `data != NULL`, `nbytes > 0` | copy to stack scratch in chunks of ≤4410, process, deliver, one drop |
| `nbytes` odd | malformed S16 fragment: log, drop, no sample invented |

No heap allocation, no `pa_threaded_mainloop_lock`, no `audio_play()` inside the callback. The
sample count and order delivered to `screen_rec_put_audio_samples()` and
`dsp_put_audio_samples()` are preserved.

## 12. What this does not claim

- It does not claim the processed audio is **audible** anywhere: it goes to the Recorder and
  the decoders only.
- It does not claim a noise-reduction quality comparable to any other radio; no such
  comparison was measured.
- It does not claim a CPU, memory or battery figure: the only process-time numbers are host
  x86_64 wall-clock measurements, and the R16 cost is `BLOCKED`.
- It does not claim a latency for the RX→speaker path; the only latency stated here is the
  algorithmic one measured **inside the capture copy**: 882 samples (20 ms) with NR on —
  441 from the frame machine plus 441 from `libspeexdsp` — and 0 with NR off.
- It does not claim the old 256-sample/48 kHz constants were merely suboptimal: they were
  wrong for a 44.1 kHz stream, and #345 measured the truncation they caused.
